# 01 · Cognitive Goals

## The five goals

Every e-learning project in this system serves one of five cognitive goals. Name the goal first. Format follows function.

1. **KNOW IT** · build retention of facts, vocabulary, codes, citations, procedures-as-recall
2. **UNDERSTAND IT** · build mental models, see structure, grasp principles, explain how something works
3. **DO IT** · build procedural skill that transfers to real performance
4. **DECIDE IT** · build judgment, see trade-offs, choose well under uncertainty
5. **BECOME IT** · build identity, internalize values, take on a perspective

## Why these five

The taxonomy comes from a working synthesis of Bloom's revised taxonomy, Merrill's component display theory, and decades of corporate L&D practice. Bloom's six levels (remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create) are useful for objective writing but cluster into broader format-determining categories at the design level.

The five-goal taxonomy is operational, not theoretical. Each goal produces a different family of formats:

- **KNOW IT** produces drill and review formats (flashcards, spaced repetition)
- **UNDERSTAND IT** produces explainer formats (visual reference, linear lessons, conceptual maps)
- **DO IT** produces practice formats (worked examples, simulations, performance demos)
- **DECIDE IT** produces judgment formats (scenarios, walk-throughs, case studies)
- **BECOME IT** produces identity formats (narratives, reflection)

If a project doesn't fit cleanly into one goal, that is a signal to slow down and ask whether it is one project or several.

## How to identify the goal

The fastest diagnostic is the verb in the learner's expected outcome.

| Learner's verb | Cognitive Goal |
|---|---|
| recall, name, recite, identify by sight, list | Know It |
| explain, describe, model, map, understand how, predict why | Understand It |
| perform, build, calibrate, operate, demonstrate, execute | Do It |
| decide, weigh, choose, diagnose, troubleshoot, evaluate | Decide It |
| internalize, embody, see differently, become, identify as | Become It |

When the verb is fuzzy ("understand" used loosely, "know" applied to a procedure), probe with: *Can you give me an example of what success looks like? What would the learner say or do?*

The example reveals the real goal. "Understand TCAS" might mean recall the operating modes (Know It), explain the theory of operation (Understand It), or perform the troubleshooting procedure (Do It). Three different goals, three different formats.

## What each goal demands of the format

### KNOW IT

The format must support **retrieval practice**. The learner has to attempt recall before reading the answer. Tests are not assessments; they are the learning event. A format that lets the learner read the answer before attempting recall has failed the goal.

Mastery looks like: the learner produces the fact under pressure without lookup.

### UNDERSTAND IT

The format must support **building mental models**. The structure of the information has to be visible, not just listed. A concept map, a comparison matrix, a timeline, or a worked example with reasoning visible. A bulleted summary of the same content is not understanding; it is exposure.

Mastery looks like: the learner explains the topic to someone else without notes.

### DO IT

The format must support **deliberate practice with feedback**. The learner attempts, sees the gap between attempt and expert performance, and tries again. A format that only shows expert performance without learner production has failed the goal.

Mastery looks like: the learner performs the skill at the field criterion without coaching.

### DECIDE IT

The format must support **judgment under varying conditions**. Multiple cases. Real consequences. A format that gives the learner one case and one right answer is teaching a procedure, not judgment.

Mastery looks like: the learner reads a novel situation correctly and chooses a defensible path.

### BECOME IT

The format must support **identification with a perspective**. The learner has to inhabit the role, feel the stakes, take on the frame. A format that tells the learner what to become is preaching; a format that lets the learner experience becoming is teaching.

Mastery looks like: the learner makes a different decision after the module than they would have before, in a real-world context that the module did not specifically anticipate.

## Goal versus format

A common error: confusing the goal with the format. The goal is what the learner needs to be able to do. The format is the vehicle that gets them there.

- KNOW IT is the goal. Flash drill is the format. Spaced review is also a format that serves the same goal.
- DECIDE IT is the goal. Scenario branch, walk-through, case study, and comparative analysis are all formats that serve this goal.

The catalog (`../reference/catalog.md`) lists 16 archetypal formats. Each one belongs to a goal. Some goals have more format options because the goal admits more variety; others have fewer because the goal is more narrowly served.

## When a project lives in two goals

Most real training projects live primarily in one goal but touch a second. A CAET module on TCAS troubleshooting might be primarily DO IT (perform the diagnostic procedure) with secondary KNOW IT (recall the resolution advisory codes). When that happens, the project is a blend. Blends are documented in the catalog. Picking the primary goal first is still the right move; the secondary goal becomes a supporting layer.

When the project is genuinely two co-equal goals, it is usually two projects. The training-architect's interview script catches this in Phase 1 by asking what success looks like; if the answer names two unrelated outcomes, the architect surfaces it.

## What this taxonomy does NOT do

It does not predict difficulty. A KNOW IT project on FAA citations can be harder than a DECIDE IT project on customer escalation, or vice versa. Difficulty comes from content volume, audience expertise, and stakes, not from goal type.

It does not order goals by sophistication. KNOW IT is not "lower" than UNDERSTAND IT, and BECOME IT is not "higher" than DO IT. Each goal serves a real need. A repair shop technician who cannot recall the right MEL citation has failed at a knowledge task; the goal is not beneath them.

It does not capture every possible learning outcome. Affective changes (motivation, attitude shift) sit awkwardly between BECOME IT and the others. Habit formation falls into DO IT but has long-tail characteristics not all DO IT formats address. The taxonomy is a working tool, not a complete theory.
